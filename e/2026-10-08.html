The Filter: From Compostable Promises to Combat Sports

Sustainability hits a corporate snag, athletes get a caffeinated edge, and the specialty community honors its ubiquitous connectors.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch Editor in Chief

October 8, 2026

Big coffee often promises the moon, but earthly realities tend to get in the way. Case in point: Nespresso recently pulled the plug on its highly touted compostable capsules, ultimately citing concerns over cup quality. It is a stark reminder that massive scale and environmental sustainability remain uneasy bedfellows. Fortunately, the true pulse of the coffee industry beats far away from corporate boardrooms. This week, we are celebrating the individuals who actively knit the global specialty network together. That includes new Sprudge Twenty inductee Esteban Cristancho of Elevation Coffee Roasters, alongside figures like Amalie Gagné-Acoulon. Amalie's omnipresent work—spanning origin farms, global competitions, and language teaching—proves that human connection is still our most vital infrastructure.

Beyond the farm and the cafe floor, coffee continues to serve as a fascinating lens for human behavior. A new study published in Nature backs up what many fighters already suspected: low-dose caffeinated coffee measurably boosts energy systems and repeated sprint indices in combat athletes. Yet, while a quick brew might improve physical output, it can also expose our psychological blind spots. The Conversation recently unpacked how the viral "iced coffee test" during job interviews serves no actual purpose in predicting candidate performance, acting instead as a glaring indicator of hiring bias.

On the consumer front, utility is getting a stylish upgrade. While everyday deal-hunters navigate the predictable chaos of Prime Day espresso discounts and gear critics debate the exact hardware required for the perfect Americano, La Marzocco has teamed up with Danish rainwear brand Rains. Their resulting "coffee-proof" apron seamlessly marries Scandinavian minimalism with Italian espresso heritage. Whether we are chasing peak athletic performance, fighting workplace bias, or just trying to keep our shirts clean on bar, coffee remains our favorite daily testing ground.
