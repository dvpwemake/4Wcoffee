The Supply Chain Gut Check

The specialty coffee industry is facing a biological and ethical reckoning.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch Editor in Chief

November 10, 2026

There is a growing urgency to bridge the widening gap between the boardroom and the barista. This week, Dutch Bros CEO Christine Barone completed a hands-on “broista” bootcamp, an attempt to ground C-suite strategy in the $7.5 billion chain’s customer-facing culture. But while corporate leaders seek authenticity at the espresso machine, the broader industry is quietly slipping on its ethical commitments. The Global Coffee Platform (GCP) recently reported a troubling backslide: only 69% of green coffee purchased by 14 major roasters met baseline sustainability standards this year, a sharp drop from 75% the previous year.

This hesitance to invest in sustainable sourcing arrives just as regulatory walls close in. The impending rollout of stringent EU green rules is threatening Vietnam’s long-standing era of “cheap coffee,” forcing producers to pivot toward higher quality and stricter compliance to survive. The squeeze is being felt across the ecosystem, even among grassroots innovators. After five grueling years of development, the team behind I’m Not A Barista announced that Batch #3 will be the final production run of the MOMENTEM hand grinder, a sobering reminder of the physical and financial toll of independent coffee hardware manufacturing.

Yet, as the industry grapples with these macro structural shifts, our biological relationship with the beverage is coming into clearer focus. New clinical research reveals that regular coffee consumption goes far beyond a caffeine buzz, fundamentally altering gut bacteria diversity and digestive physiology. Coffee physically rewrites our internal ecosystems, much like modern cafes—such as those actively pioneering the intersection of specialty coffee and health-conscious food menus—are reshaping our daily rituals. As we work to preserve the aesthetic soul of our spaces, championed by studios like San Francisco’s Neon Works keeping vintage cafe signs glowing, the mandate is clear. We must ensure the health of our global supply chains matches the vitality of the culture we serve.
