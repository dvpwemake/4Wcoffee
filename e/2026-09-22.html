Shaking the Grounds

Science, Stance, and Sanctuary

By Dr. Wallace Lynch Editor in Chief

September 22, 2026

As extraction methods evolve and unexpected machines earn top marks, the coffee industry is finding its voice on global politics and celebrating the human stories behind the bar.

The specialty coffee community can no longer afford to exist in a vacuum. This reality is brought to the forefront today by CoffeeGeek’s impassioned editorial, which urges coffee professionals and enthusiasts alike to take a definitive stand against fascist policies reshaping our world. Coffee has always been inherently political, and as institutions like Ritual Coffee Roasters unveil their latest stunning build-out in San Francisco, we are reminded that our cafes are the very community hubs where these vital societal conversations take root.

On the bar, we are witnessing a rigorous, joyful re-examination of our mechanics. Barista Hustle explores Lance Hedrick’s recent revelation that simply shaking espresso grounds can dramatically boost extraction, challenging long-held distribution dogmas. They also delve into the potent legacy of the coffee overdose—the famed triple ristretto—with 2024 World Barista Champion Mikael Jasin. Even our gear expectations are being delightfully upended. CoffeeGeek's review of the $549 Ninja Luxe Café Premier proves that what some initially dismissed as a consumer toy is actually an 88.5-scoring powerhouse, proving innovation is becoming more accessible. And for those seeking a lighter refreshment amidst the heavy science, Cascara Cabinet is sparking a fizzy RTD cascara renaissance.

Yet, beneath the high-extraction science and political discourse, coffee remains intimately human. Not a Barista captures this ethos perfectly in two new profiles. They trace Lonika Tay’s inspiring ascent from weighing beans and sealing bags in an Indonesian roastery to commanding the international competition stage. We also get a glimpse into the beautiful chaos of Angel Kotsifakis’s coffee lab, where the intense focus of brewing is punctuated by the gleeful shouts of his child. Seeing a small bicycle nestled among gleaming espresso equipment is a poignant reminder: the Fourth Wave isn’t just about the perfect cup—it’s about honoring the real, complex lives of the people pouring them.
