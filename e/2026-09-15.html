Named for the world

Buon Ma Thuot. Farms that held.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch Editor in Chief

September 15, 2026

In Buon Ma Thuot, in Vietnam’s coffee highlands, the World Coffee Museum tells a global story. Not a Vietnam museum. World. Jens Burg spent two decades collecting. More than 10,000 artifacts moved from Hamburg after Dang Le Nguyen Vu of Trung Nguyen acquired the collection. The building takes the longhouse of the Ede. Coffee as shared heritage, from a producing nation.

On Tuesday, September 15, 2026, we hold that naming to one pressure: whether the cup still belongs to the farm when the season is sold in a box, and when a pitcher has to be split so both drinks get the foam.

Puerto Rico was once the world’s seventh largest producer. About 2,000 farms now. Irma and Maria took up to 90 percent of the plants. They held. Jamaica’s Blue Mountain is less than 0.01 percent of world supply. About 80 percent of those farms sit under five acres. Japan still takes most of it. Sprudge says pumpkin spice is dead. Titan Caskets sells an orange box for sixteen hundred dollars. PSL season does not have to end when you do. Barista Hustle teaches how to split a pitcher so both drinks get the same foam. Four quarters. Pour three, return one. Nutation packs the puck denser. Coarse particles fall. Some of those boulders may only have been clumps. CoffeeGeek says the grinder is still the first tool. Twelve budget picks. A Kingrinder at 45 dollars. An Encore that will not retire. Moderate coffee, three to four cups, still looks after the brain and the heart.

A museum named for the world. Farms that held. The fourth wave is not the casket for the season. It is whether the cup still belongs to the farm that grew it.
