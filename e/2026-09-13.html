Already there

Coffee since six, a new room in Copenhagen.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch Editor in Chief

September 13, 2026

For Andrés Paparoni, coffee was never something he found later. Growing up in Venezuela, it was family. His father, Gustavo, a Q grader and teacher. His grandfather sold Gaggia. He drank it from six. At fourteen he was behind the bar with his father. Not every branch of the family stayed in coffee. Enough of it stayed to shape the room he grew up in. A Gardelli Panama and a Sasa Sestic routine made him want to train, compete, stay. He is now a Spanish barista champion, instructing at Nomad in Barcelona. Origin was not a tasting note. It was the house.

On Sunday, September 13, 2026, we hold that house to one pressure: who the cup is for when the bag is fresh from a village, and when the scale does every job.

Sprudge restocked the Roaster's Village. A weekly list because people keep asking where to buy. Sprudge put Fresh Pots in Copenhagen on the Build-Outs. A new cafe. A new counter. CoffeeGeek put a Laybird MagAttach on the bench. Sixty nine dollars. Magnets. One tiny scale for espresso and pour over, no app, a live ratio in red. Option-O's Lagom Casa is the other all rounder: slow conical burrs, a magnetic cup, a knocker, espresso and filter without a second machine. Jonathan Gagné's Physics of Espresso is out, graphs and drawings, sold through Scott Rao. I'M NOT A BARISTA took charity wristbands, L1GHT cups, and a MOMENTEM grinder to Bangkok. Not only to show the work.

A life that was already coffee. A bag that arrived this week. A room that just opened. The fourth wave is not the scale that does many jobs. It is whether the cup still belongs to the house that had it from the start.
