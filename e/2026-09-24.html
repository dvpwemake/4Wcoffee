Shifts at the Source and the Pursuit of Extraction Perfection

By Dr. Wallace Lynch Editor in Chief

September 24, 2026

As one LA roaster radically rethinks the supply chain, we explore Nepal’s rising terroir, the extremes of bean-by-bean grinding, and a barista champion’s national TV debut.

The structural norms of specialty coffee's supply chain are undergoing significant shifts. Los Angeles specialty coffee company Go Get Em Tiger has launched a "Roasted At Origin" initiative, with plans to move 20% of its roasting operations directly to producing countries by the end of 2026, scaling to an ambitious 70% by the end of 2027. This push to retain value at the source parallels the continuous expansion of the global coffee map, highlighting compelling new origins like Nepal. While the landlocked nation lacks the established recognition of Colombia or Ethiopia, its rapid evolution and unique terroir make it an increasingly vital player in the specialty industry.

On the bar, the technical pursuit of the perfect cup continues—sometimes bordering on the absurd. A recent dive by Barista Hustle explored grinding coffee bean-by-bean, categorizing it alongside cryogenic grinding as a form of "impractical perfection" that is generally dismissed as inappropriate for commercial environments. A more practical pursuit of perfection can be found in the newest wave of high-extraction espresso filter baskets, where manufacturers have completely re-thought bottom hole patterns to ensure even extraction.

Manual brewing and travel gear are also seeing clever innovations. CoffeeGeek recently detailed a hack to build a fantastic, full-immersion, no-bypass brewer using two inexpensive Hario devices—the Mugen and the Switch. For brewers on the go, the new Outin Mino travel espresso maker promises a smaller footprint, though The Coffee Chronicler notes it represents two steps forward, but one step back where it really matters.

In community news, the organization Not a Barista is testing a new Shopify storefront to provide a smoother shopping experience and clearer product information for its supporters. Finally, specialty coffee continues its crossover into mainstream pop culture, with US Barista Champion Laila Ghambari recently appearing as a contestant on the nationally televised game show The Floor.
